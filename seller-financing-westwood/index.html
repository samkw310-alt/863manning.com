Seller financing on a Westwood home

With seller financing, the seller acts as the lender for part or all of the price. It can open doors when bank loans are hard to get or slow. Here's how it generally works in California.

What is seller financing?

Seller financing, also called owner financing, seller carryback or "owner will carry," means the seller lends you part of the purchase price. You sign a promissory note to the seller, secured by a deed of trust on the property, and make payments to the seller instead of (or in addition to) a bank.

Common structures

  • Bank first loan + seller second. A conventional or jumbo lender makes the first loan and the seller carries a smaller second. The first lender has to allow it.
  • Seller carries a larger share. The seller finances most of the balance after your down payment, often with a balloon payment after a few years.
  • Short-term bridge. The seller carries for a limited period while you sell another property or refinance.

Terms you'll negotiate

  • Down payment
  • Interest rate and payment schedule
  • Loan length and any balloon payment
  • Prepayment terms
  • What happens if a payment is late

The paperwork

A seller-financed purchase normally closes through escrow with title insurance, like any other sale. The note and deed of trust are recorded, and the buyer's ability to repay is evaluated. In California, when an arranger of credit (such as a real estate agent) is involved in seller financing on a 1–4 unit home, a written seller financing disclosure statement is generally required (Cal. Civ. Code §2956 and following). Federal and state lending rules can also apply, so buyer and seller should each use their own attorney.

Questions & answers

What does 'owner will carry' mean?

It means the seller may be willing to finance part of the purchase price. The buyer signs a promissory note secured by a deed of trust and makes payments to the seller.

Can I combine a bank loan with seller financing?

Often, if the first-mortgage lender allows a seller second. Whether it works depends on the lender's guidelines, your qualifications and the seller's approval.
