Lease option to buy a home in Los Angeles: how rent-to-own works

A lease option lets you move into a home now and buy it later at a price agreed today. It can help if you need time to sell another property, build income history, or line up a loan. Here's how it generally works.

What is a lease option?

A lease option combines two agreements: a lease of the home and an option to buy it. You pay the seller an option fee for the right, but not the obligation, to purchase at an agreed price within an agreed period. If you decide not to buy, you generally walk away and the seller keeps the option fee.

The key terms you'll negotiate

  • Purchase price. Usually fixed up front, so you know what you'll pay later.
  • Option fee. Paid at the start. Whether it is credited toward the price, and whether any of it is refundable, depends entirely on the agreement.
  • Term. How long you have to exercise the option, often one to three years.
  • Rent and rent credit. Monthly rent, and whether any part of it is credited toward the purchase.
  • Repairs and maintenance. Who handles what while you're living there.
  • How you exercise the option. Written notice deadlines and closing timelines.

Lease option vs. lease-purchase

With a lease option you have the right to buy but don't have to. With a lease-purchase you agree up front that you will buy, so walking away can mean breaching the contract. Make sure you know which one you're signing.

Who a lease option can suit

  • Buyers who need to sell another home first
  • Self-employed buyers building the income history lenders want
  • Buyers relocating to Los Angeles who want to live in the area before committing
  • Buyers waiting on a bonus, equity event or 1031 exchange timing

Risks to watch

  • You can lose the option fee and any rent credit if you don't buy in time.
  • You'll usually still need a mortgage or cash at the end, so talk to a lender early.
  • Run a title search at the start, and ask your attorney about recording a memorandum of option to protect your rights.
  • Get every term in writing, and have a California real estate attorney review the agreement.

Questions & answers

Is rent-to-own available on higher-end homes in Los Angeles?

Sometimes. It depends on the seller. The seller of 863 Manning Ave in Westwood, offered at $4,650,000, will consider lease option proposals, subject to approval.

Is the option fee refundable?

Usually not, but it depends on the written agreement. Many agreements credit the option fee toward the price if you buy.

Does my rent count toward the purchase?

Only if the agreement says so. Any rent credit is negotiated between buyer and seller.

Do I still need a mortgage at the end of a lease option?

Usually yes, unless you pay cash or the seller agrees to carry financing. Talk to a lender early so you know what you'll need to qualify.

What is the difference between a lease option and a lease-purchase?

A lease option gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy. A lease-purchase obligates you to buy on the agreed terms.
